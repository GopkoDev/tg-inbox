# Changelog — broadcasts

## broadcasts — any member drafts a plain-text broadcast for a tag audience or a hand-picked group, a business owner launches it to a frozen recipient list, sending is paced below the bot's limit and gives way to replies, and a live report shows per recipient who got it, who failed and who blocked the bot

**What:** A workspace can now message a chosen part of its contact base from its own bot, without leaving the panel, and the bot learns who blocked it.

- **Broadcasts page.** Drafts and launched broadcasts together, the most recently changed first, each with its name, status (draft, sending, sent, stopped), audience size and who last changed or launched it; statuses update live. Any member creates, edits and duplicates drafts and sees every broadcast and report of the workspace; a draft is deleted by its author or a business owner after a confirmation naming its audience size.
- **The draft.** An internal name (1–80 characters, never sent) and plain text (1–4096 characters counted the way Telegram counts them, trimmed at both ends, with the remaining length shown while typing; no formatting, no placeholders). Edits save as you go against the version you saw: a teammate's newer save is never overwritten — you are told and shown the current version.
- **Tag audience.** The Contacts filter's "any of", "all of" or "no tags" (no tag = every contact), plus tags to leave out. A renamed tag keeps working; a deleted or merged tag drops out with a notice.
- **Hand-picked group.** Filled from the Contacts search and tag filter, one contact at a time or "add all matching" (it says how many it will add, and adds the current ones if the match changed meanwhile), and trimmed. A contact is in a group once; additions and removals by different members are all kept and never make a teammate's text save stale. A group belongs to its draft only. At most 10 000 contacts per audience (reachable or not); a larger tag audience can be saved but not launched.
- **Who it would reach.** Every draft and the launch confirmation show the same breakdown: audience size, left out because they blocked the bot, left out because they never wrote to the current bot, and the number it would reach.
- **Launch (business owners only).** A confirmation shows the exact text as the customer sees it, the number it will be sent to, the left-out counts and a warning that unexpected messages can get the bot blocked or reported, which limits it for support too. Confirming freezes the recipient list to exactly the contacts counted; if the text, the audience or the reachable set changed since the confirmation opened (even at the same number), nothing launches and the current version is shown. A double click or two owners at once launch once. At most 10 launches per workspace in any 24 hours; a draft that reaches nobody, or a workspace without a working bot, cannot launch (with the way to the bot settings). Operators see no Launch and a saved `/launch` link lands on the draft.
- **Sending.** At most 25 messages per second for all of a bot's broadcasts together, leaving room under Telegram's ~30 for replies, pings and the bot's own answers; replies never wait for broadcasts. Two broadcasts of a workspace take turns. On any rate-limit answer the broadcasts wait for the bot's send pause and continue without skipping or doubling anyone. Each recipient is sent the broadcast at most once: a send that got no answer (including one in flight when the service stopped) ends "not confirmed" and is never repeated; any other refusal ends that recipient as failed with its reason. Telegram refusing the text, or the bot stopping working, being disconnected or replaced, ends the broadcast at once with every waiting recipient failed with the reason; nothing of it is sent later.
- **Stop (business owners only).** A confirmation says how many recipients are still waiting and that a stop cannot be undone; waiting recipients become "not sent — stopped", a send in flight finishes and is recorded, and the broadcast ends "stopped" with who stopped it and when.
- **Report.** Live totals (waiting, delivered, blocked the bot, failed) and each recipient with their outcome, time and a plain reason ("account no longer exists", "not confirmed — Telegram did not answer, not sent again", …). A launched broadcast is read-only and offers to duplicate it.
- **Duplicate.** "Copy of …" (cut to fit 80 characters) with the same text and audience; for an ended broadcast either "all recipients" or "only recipients it did not reach" (failed for a reason a new attempt can fix, stopped before their turn, and the "not confirmed" ones only after a warning).
- **Blocked the bot.** The bot now receives Telegram's block / unblock notices (`my_chat_member`) and also learns a block from a refused reply or broadcast. The Contacts list marks the row and the profile says "Blocked the bot since …", live; unblocking or writing again clears it. Blocking creates no message, opens no conversation, moves no unread count and triggers no ping. Marks belong to the Telegram bot connected now: reconnecting the same bot keeps them, connecting a different bot starts over (and contacts who never wrote to it are left out of launches).
- **In the inbox.** Each delivered (or "not confirmed") broadcast shows in the recipient's thread as its own kind of item — text, broadcast name and the owner who launched it — at its send time. It never reopens, assigns or marks the conversation, and moves no unread, attention count or ping. A customer's answer is an ordinary customer message.

**Why:** Pilot workspaces hold a tagged contact base pitched as "your broadcast base in the making", and broadcasts are the main reason to pay that v1 lacked ([spec](../spec.md) §1). The sharpest risk is that broadcasts and support share one bot, so launch is owner-only, confirmed against a count, capped per day, paced below the limit and stoppable (spec §6.1). Key decisions:

- [ADR-0001](../adr/0001-add-a-broadcasts-module-that-owns-drafts-groups-and-recipients-and-reaches-other-modules-through-their-app-layers.md): a new `broadcasts` module owns drafts, groups and recipients and reaches other modules through their app layers.
- [ADR-0002](../adr/0002-keep-per-bot-reachability-of-contacts-in-contacts-keyed-by-the-telegram-bot-id.md): per-bot reachability (wrote / blocked) lives in `contacts`, keyed by the Telegram bot id.
- [ADR-0003](../adr/0003-learn-blocks-from-my-chat-member-updates-and-refused-sends-in-bots-and-re-register-outdated-webhooks-in-the-health-check.md): blocks come from `my_chat_member` and refused sends; the health check re-registers outdated webhooks.
- [ADR-0004](../adr/0004-evaluate-audiences-in-contacts-with-the-contacts-filter-rules-and-return-the-breakdown-and-the-reachable-set.md): audiences are evaluated in `contacts` with the Contacts filter rules.
- [ADR-0005](../adr/0005-version-draft-fields-and-keep-the-group-as-per-contact-rows-outside-the-version.md): draft fields are versioned; group rows sit outside the version.
- [ADR-0006](../adr/0006-launch-against-the-shown-version-and-an-audience-fingerprint-under-a-per-workspace-launch-lock.md): launch against the shown version and an audience fingerprint under a per-workspace lock.
- [ADR-0007](../adr/0007-send-through-a-per-workspace-pg-boss-chain-that-claims-each-recipient-before-its-send.md): sending is a per-workspace pg-boss chain that claims each recipient before its send (at most once).
- [ADR-0008](../adr/0008-pace-broadcasts-at-25-per-second-per-bot-in-turns-and-wait-for-the-send-pause.md): 25 per second per bot, in turns, waiting for the send pause.
- [ADR-0009](../adr/0009-end-stop-or-abort-a-broadcast-by-settling-every-waiting-recipient-in-one-transaction.md): end, stop or abort settles every waiting recipient in one transaction.
- [ADR-0010](../adr/0010-store-broadcast-items-as-a-conversations-thread-entry-that-never-touches-conversation-state.md): broadcast items are a thread entry that never touches conversation state.
- [ADR-0011](../adr/0011-push-broadcast-changes-as-id-only-signals-coalesced-to-once-per-second-and-page-the-report.md): id-only live signals, report changes coalesced to once per second, the report paged.
- [ADR-0012](../adr/0012-count-broadcast-text-in-utf-16-code-units-after-trimming-with-one-shared-rule.md): text counted in UTF-16 code units after trimming, one shared rule.

**How to use:** In the panel open *Broadcasts* → *New broadcast*, name it, write the text, choose a tag audience or build a group (*Add contacts*), and, as a business owner, *Launch* → confirm; follow the report on the broadcast's page, *Stop* while it sends, or *Duplicate*. The HTTP surface is in [openapi.yaml](../contracts/openapi.yaml), all under `/api/workspaces/:workspaceId/broadcasts`:

- `GET` / `POST …/broadcasts` — the list; create a draft. `GET …/:broadcastId` — one broadcast.
- `POST …/:broadcastId/save` (name / text, with `expected_version`), `…/audience` (kind + tags), `…/delete`, `…/duplicate`.
- `GET …/:broadcastId/group` (+ `/membership`, `/matching`), `POST …/group/contacts`, `…/group/contacts/:contactId/remove`, `…/group/add-matching`.
- Owner-only: `GET …/:broadcastId/launch-confirmation`, `POST …/:broadcastId/launch` (the confirmation's `version` + `fingerprint`), `POST …/:broadcastId/stop`.
- `GET …/:broadcastId/recipients` — the report pages; `GET /api/workspaces/:workspaceId/inbox/conversations/:conversationId/broadcast-items/:broadcastItemId` — one thread item.
- Refusals: eleven new `BROADCAST_*` error codes in `packages/shared/src/errors.ts` (name / text invalid, draft or group changed, not a draft, not ended, audience changed or too large, no reachable contact, needs a working bot, launch limit reached).
- Live: `broadcast.changed`, `contact.changed` with `change: 'reach'`, and `conversation.changed` with item type `broadcast` on the `GET …/inbox/live` socket, ids only ([events.md](../contracts/events.md)). Tabs opened before the deploy ignore them until their next read.

**Operational notes:**

- **Migrations:** nine reversible migrations, `1791681110012_create_contact_bot_reach` … `1791681110020_add_bots_needs_token_at` (per-bot reach and its backfill, a `(workspace_id, id)` key on conversations, `broadcasts`, `broadcast_audience_tags`, `broadcast_group_contacts`, `broadcast_recipients`, `broadcast_items`, `bots.needs_token_at`; see [data-model.md](../data-model.md)). Run `pnpm db:migrate` on deploy before the new API starts. The backfill treats every existing contact as having written to its workspace's current Telegram bot (exact for pilots, who have not changed bots).
- **After the deploy:** each bot's next health check (≤ 3 minutes) re-registers its webhook with `my_chat_member` in its allowed updates — no owner action. Until then a block is still learned from a refused send.
- **Jobs (pg-boss):** three new queues created by `buildApp()` — `broadcasts.send` (policy `stately`, one chain per workspace, retry 3, expire 60 s), its dead letter `broadcasts.send-dlq` (restarts the chain 60 s later) and `broadcasts.measure-blocked-share` (48 h after a launch, logs the blocked share for the §7 alert, to the tg-inbox team's monitoring only).
- **Rollback:** revert the deploy, then `pnpm db:rollback` nine times. This drops every broadcast, group, recipient outcome, broadcast thread item and blocked mark (contacts, conversations and messages stay). Webhooks re-registered with `my_chat_member` keep receiving it; the old code ignores it as an unsupported update.
- **Config / feature flag:** none — pace, link length and coalescing are constants in `broadcasts/domain`. **Tenancy:** no new controlled exception; every cross-entity link carries `workspace_id` in a composite FK.
- **Logs:** broadcast text, names and chat ids never appear in logs, frames or job payloads. Run-book integrity queries: `broadcastIntegrity.*` in `apps/api/src/e2e/helpers.ts` — nine queries (recipients left `sending` with no chain, a recipient with two items, recipients added after launch, launches over the cap, frozen counts that differ, contacts across workspaces, outcomes without their item and items without their outcome, waiting recipients on an ended broadcast), all expect 0 rows.
- **Residual notes from the review:** freezing 10 000 recipients takes about 0.5 s on an idle machine and 3–4 s under full-suite load against the 2 s budget (machine load, not code; dropping the redundant plain `workspace_id` FK of `broadcast_recipients` would save about a quarter — a schema follow-up); the `reply` and `activity` thread cursors accept any non-empty id (pre-existing).
- **Open questions carried:** OQ-1 (limit audiences to recent or opted-in contacts) and OQ-3 (group answers to a broadcast) are due after the first 30 days of pilot broadcasts. OQ-4 (how a workspace removes a contact) was due before this step shipped and stays open on its default: no in-product deletion, the tg-inbox team erases by hand on request.

**Acceptance criteria delivered:** AC-01 – AC-18 — the tag-audience draft and its breakdown (AC-01), the name and text rules (AC-02), no overwritten saves and kept group edits (AC-03), the hand-picked group (AC-04), the 10 000 limit and one contact per group (AC-05), the confirmed launch of a frozen list, once (AC-06), owner-only launch and author-or-owner delete (AC-07), access control and tenancy (AC-08), read-only after launch, no empty launch, 10 launches per 24 h (AC-09), the bot requirement and the current bot (AC-10), the live delivery report and at most once per recipient (AC-11), ending on a refused text or a lost bot (AC-12), duplicates (AC-13), blocked the bot (AC-14), replies and other workspaces unaffected, pacing and turns (AC-15), the broadcast item in the thread (AC-16), exactly one plain-text message per recipient (AC-17), stop (AC-18).
